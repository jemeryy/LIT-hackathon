Compiled 2026-09-05 (SGT). All facts cited to source URL. Anything not verified is flagged explicitly.

## 1. SCT jurisdiction

**Primary source:** Singapore Statutes Online, Small Claims Tribunals Act 1984, current version as at 5 Sep 2026 — https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/SCTA1984

Section numbers confirmed directly from the SSO table of contents for the Act (Part 2 "ESTABLISHMENT AND JURISDICTION OF TRIBUNALS"):
- **s 5** — Jurisdiction of tribunal
- **s 6** — Exclusion of other jurisdictions
- **s 7** — Transfer of claims
- **s 8** — No division of claims
- **s 9** — Abandonment of part of claim to give tribunal jurisdiction
- **s 15** — Lodging claims
- **s 16** — Contents of claim
- **s 23** — Right of audience
- Part 3: **s 38** — Appeal on point of law; **s 39** — Powers of General Division of High Court on appeal
- Part 4: **s 40** — Order of tribunal to be final; **s 41** — Setting aside of order; **s 42** — Appeal does not operate as stay of execution or enforcement
- **THE SCHEDULE** — "Specified claims"

Definitions section (s 2(1)), quoted verbatim from SSO (full text loaded successfully for ss 1–2 only — see caveat below):
> "prescribed extended limit" means $30,000 or such other sum as the Minister may, after consultation with the Chief Justice, prescribe by order in the Gazette;
> "prescribed limit" means $20,000 or such other sum as the Minister may, after consultation with the Chief Justice, prescribe by order in the Gazette;
> "specified claim" means a claim specified in the Schedule;
> "work order" means an order to rectify a defect in goods or any damage to property, or to make good any deficiency in the performance of services, by doing such work or attending to such matters (including the replacement of goods or parts thereof) as may be specified in the order.

Also from s 2(2), value of a claim relating to a contract:
> (a) rescission of the contract — the value of the contract;
> (b) recovery of a progress payment due under the contract — the value of the contract;
> (c) any other case — the quantum of the claim.

**Caveat:** SSO's page (https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/SCTA1984) renders sections 1 and 2 in full on initial load; the remaining sections (3 onward, including s 5's jurisdiction wording and the Schedule text) load dynamically and could not be retrieved as plain text by my fetch tools (the SSO print/PDF endpoints returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches). I could not obtain verbatim SSO text of s 5 or the Schedule. The claim-limit and time-bar figures below are independently corroborated by the State Courts' own official guide (a primary source, see §4), so I'm confident in the figures, just not able to quote the Act's own wording for s 5/the Schedule verbatim.

### Claim limit
- Standard limit: **$20,000**.
- With both parties' consent (uploading a Memorandum of Consent in the prescribed format): **$30,000**.
- Source (official State Courts guide, primary): "A Guide to Small Claims" PDF, State Courts, p.4 — https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/docs/default-source/civil-docs/sct_guide_to_small_claims.pdf — "$20,000 OR $30,000* … *If both parties consent by uploading a memorandum of consent in such format as prescribed by the Registrar." Claims cannot be split/divided to fit under the limit (s 8, "No division of claims" — title only verified; substantive text not retrieved).

### Time bar
- **2 years** from the date the cause of action accrued.
- Source: same Guide, p.4: "All claims must be filed within two years after the date the cause of action accrued. A cause of action is the fact or facts which entitles you, in law, to start a court action against the Respondent. You may file a claim in the civil courts, e.g. at the Magistrates' Courts if the cause of action accrued more than 2 years ago."

### Claim types covered (per judiciary.gov.sg "Cases eligible for a small claim" — https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/civil/cases-eligible-small-claim — and the State Courts Guide p.3, both corroborating each other)
1. Contract for sale of goods
2. Contract for provision of services (e.g. renovation)
3. Tenancy for **residential** premises not exceeding 2 years (commercial/industrial leases excluded)
4. Wrongful damage to property — **excluding** damage from a motor vehicle accident, and excluding damage to movable property caused by a neighbour
5. Unfair practice by a supplier under the Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act, involving a contract for sale of goods, provision of services, or a hire-purchase agreement
6. Motor vehicle deposit refund claims under Consumer Protection regulations
7. Other statutory claims under written law, e.g.: Town Council recovering service and conservancy charges; MCST recovering management/sinking fund contributions; HDB recovering outstanding improvement contribution

### Exclusions (per judiciary.gov.sg and the Guide)
- Claim exceeds $30,000
- Respondent is outside Singapore (claims cannot be served on anyone outside Singapore)
- Motor vehicle accident property damage
- Neighbour-caused damage to movable property
- Employment matters (these go to the separate Employment Claims Tribunals)
- Commercial/industrial tenancies, or residential tenancies exceeding 2 years

### No lawyers
- s 23 "Right of audience" (title confirmed from SSO TOC). Corroborated in the Guide, p.6: "Lawyers and agents are not allowed to represent a party in SCT proceedings before a Registrar or Tribunal Magistrate. You may be represented by a lawyer in appeal proceedings in the District Court or appeal proceedings in the General Division of the High Court."
- Exception: an individual unable to represent themselves "by reason of old age, illiteracy or a mental or health condition" can apply for an authorised representative (Guide, p.6).

## 2. Filing process on CJTS, fees, pre-filing alternatives

**Primary sources:**
- State Courts, "A Guide to Small Claims" PDF — https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/docs/default-source/civil-docs/sct_guide_to_small_claims.pdf ("All information is correct as of 1 December 2022" — the PDF's own disclaimer, so treat the fee table and process detail as current-as-of-that-date pending any later change; the $20k/$30k limit and 2-year bar are corroborated by the live judiciary.gov.sg pages fetched the same day as this research)
- https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/civil/file-small-claim
- https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/civil/how-to-file-serve-small-claim
- https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/civil/small-claims

### Filing fees (Guide p.5 table, "Filing Fee")
| Claim/counterclaim value | Individual (S$) | Other entity (S$) |
|---|---|---|
| $5,000 or below | 10 | 50 |
| Above $5,000, up to $10,000 | 20 | 100 |
| Above $10,000, up to $30,000 | 1% of amount claimed | 3% of amount claimed |

### End-to-end steps (Guide, Chapter 3, "Filing a Claim on the CJTS", p.11)
1. Go to https://cjts.judiciary.gov.sg/home
2. Login with Singpass (individuals/business entities); tourists without Singpass can apply for a CJTS Pass
3. Update "My Profile" with personal particulars
4. Click "Online Applications" → "SCT" → choose e-service Claim Form
5. Key in pre-filing ID, or do the pre-filing assessment in CJTS to get one ("Before filing a claim, please attempt a pre-filing assessment in the CJTS")
6. Complete the pre-filing assessment questions, click Submit
7. Fill in claim: Claimant details, Respondent details, upload supporting documents (PDF)
8. Pay filing fee (online by Credit Card/eNETS, or offline: generate payment advice, pay by cash/NETS/NETS FlashPay/credit card at State Courts payment kiosks, or PayNow via QR)
9. Choose a Consultation date and time
10. "Save Respondent Copy", print Notice of Consultation and claim, and **serve the respondent within 7 working days** of filing (claim cannot be served on anyone outside Singapore)

### Documents to upload (Guide p.8–9)
- Contracts and supporting documents (invoices, receipts, contracts, letters, emails/messages)
- Tenancy Agreement and Stamp Duty Certification (for tenancy disputes)
- Photographs (if applicable)
- Letter of Authorisation on company letterhead (corporate entity)
- Latest ACRA business profile of claimant/respondent (if not an individual), obtained within 1 month of filing date
- All non-English documents must be translated into English

### After filing: service, DOS, Consultation, Hearing
- Claimant must serve the printed Notice of Consultation on the respondent by Personal Delivery or Registered Post within 7 working days of filing.
- A **Declaration of Service (DOS)** with proof of service must be filed in CJTS before the first Consultation.
- **Consultation**: a proceeding before the Registrar where all parties discuss the case; the Registrar facilitates settlement. If settled, a Consent Order is made. If not, the Registrar may adjourn, direct mediation, or fix the case for Hearing. If the claim is outside SCT jurisdiction, the Registrar issues a discontinuance order. Absent parties can have a default order made against them.
- **Hearing**: before a Tribunal Magistrate if unresolved at Consultation. Each party presents case/evidence; possible orders: pay money, perform work, deliver vacant possession, dismiss claim, pay costs/disbursements. Default order possible if a party is absent.

### e-Negotiation / e-Mediation (Guide, Chapter 2, p.9–10)
- **e-Negotiation**: initiated by the respondent after filing; parties get **5 rounds of offers** each on the CJTS platform; if settled, they can apply online for a Consent Order without going to Court.
- **e-Mediation**: if both parties agree, CJTS schedules a session with a court mediator; settlement can also lead to an online Consent Order application.

### After an order / enforcement
- A Tribunal's order is "final and binding" (s 40 title, corroborated in Guide p.22).
- Orders can only be appealed "on a question of law or if the Tribunal was outside its jurisdiction" — no appeal on findings of fact (Guide p.22; s 38 "Appeal on point of law").
- Appeal route: apply to the **District Court for leave** (called "permission" for claims filed on/after 1 April 2022) via e-service "Application for Leave to Appeal" in CJTS, **within 14 days** of the Tribunal's order; if granted, appeal proceeds to the **General Division of the High Court**. Lawyers ARE allowed at this appeal stage.
- Appeal against a Registrar's discontinuance order: appeal to the Tribunal Magistrate, fee **S$20**, within 1 month of the order (Guide p.21).
- Setting aside a default order: apply via CJTS e-service, within 1 month of the order, explaining the absence.
- **Enforcement**: "An SCT order can be enforced in the same way as an order of a District Court" (Guide, p.21, footnote). Filing an appeal does not stay enforcement unless a "Stay of Enforcement Process" e-service application is filed and granted.

### Pre-filing alternatives (secondary + CASE own site)
- **CASE (Consumers Association of Singapore)** — https://www.case.org.sg/legal-options/ and https://www.case.org.sg/submit-a-complaint/ (I read these via search snippets, not a direct fetch — treat the quotes below as reported by search, not independently verified against the live page text):
  - CASE helps draft a **letter of demand**: "CASE will help you draft a letter to the retailer to communicate your concerns and your ideal outcome. You will have to deliver this letter personally to the company and CASE will not follow up on the dispute on your behalf." For CASE members, a Consumer Relations Officer corresponds with the retailer directly.
  - **Mediation**: "CASE's mediation service has a success rate of approximately 70%... Each settlement signed is legally binding in court."
  - If deadlocked, CASE advises escalation to SCT or other legal recourse.
  - **Not independently verified**: I did not manage to directly fetch case.org.sg pages (only got search-engine summaries of them). Treat exact wording as reported second-hand; the general process (demand letter → mediation → SCT) is consistent with what CASE publicly describes.

## 3. Guide on generative AI use by court users

**Primary source (full PDF read):** Registrar's Circular No. 1 of 2024, Supreme Court of Singapore — https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/docs/default-source/circulars/2024/registrar's_circular_no_1_2024_supreme_court.pdf

- **Full title**: "Guide on the Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools by Court Users"
- **Issued/dated**: 23 September 2024, signed by Tan Boon Heng, Registrar, Supreme Court
- **Effective**: 1 October 2024
- **Scope** (para 1, "Application"): applies "to all matters in the Supreme Court, the State Courts (including the Small Claims Tribunals, the Employment Claims Tribunals and the Community Disputes Resolution Tribunals) and the Family Justice Courts" — so it directly covers SCT proceedings.

Key definitions (para 2):
- "Court User" = "any person who is involved in a Court case, including prosecutors, lawyers, Self-Represented Persons, or witnesses."
- "Self-Represented Person" = "a litigant who is not legally represented... also referred to as 'litigant-in-person' or 'accused-in-person'."
- "Court Documents" includes "text, images, sounds, videos, data and any other material that is filed in or submitted to Court, such as written submissions, skeletal arguments, pleadings, affidavits and opening statements."

**General principles (para 3), quoted verbatim:**
> (1) The Court does not prohibit the use of Generative AI tools to prepare Court Documents, provided that this Guide is complied with.
> (2)(b) Where the Court User is a Self-Represented Person, he or she is also responsible for ensuring that all information provided to the Court is independently verified, accurate, true, and appropriate.
> (3) The Court maintains a neutral stance on the use of Generative AI tools. It is important to emphasise that Generative AI is a tool, and any output generated should only be used on the basis that the Court User assumes full responsibility for the output. **Unless specifically asked for by the Court, pre-emptive declaration of the use of Generative AI is not required**, as the responsibility for any resulting content ultimately rests with the Court User.

**Use rules (para 5), quoted/closely paraphrased:**
- "As a Court User, you are fully responsible for the content in all your Court Documents."
- Any AI-generated output used must: (i) be accurate; (ii) be relevant; (iii) not infringe IP rights.
- "Generative AI tools should not be used to generate any evidence that you wish to rely upon in Court" — cannot ask AI to create, fabricate, embellish, strengthen or dilute evidence. A first-cut draft of an affidavit/statement is allowed if the Guide is complied with.
- **Ensuring accuracy** — must: fact-check and proof-read AI content; edit/adapt it to the situation; verify any case law/legislation/textbook/article references actually exist and say what's attributed to them; verify using a reliable source — for Self-Represented Persons specifically named as **the eLitigation GD Viewer** (https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/judgments/judgments-case-summaries) for case law and **Singapore Statutes Online** (https://sso.agc.gov.sg/) for legislation; "it is *not* sufficient verification... to ask a Generative AI tool for confirmation... you cannot use one Generative AI tool to confirm the content generated from another."
- Must be prepared to identify which portions of Court Documents used AI content and explain how it was verified, if the Court asks.
- Must protect IP (proper source attribution) and confidentiality/personal data — "All information you provide to Generative AI chatbots may potentially be disclosed publicly."

**Declaration duties (para 5(8)):** only **if the Court has grounds to believe** AI-generated output was used, the user may be asked to: (a) inform the Court whether Generative AI tools were used; (b) declare compliance with the Guide; (c) do so by affidavit if required. (Pre-emptive/automatic declaration is explicitly not required — see para 3(3) above.)

**Consequences of non-compliance (para 5(9)):** costs ordered against the user (personally, if a lawyer); the Court may disregard the document in whole or part, or give it less weight; disciplinary action if a lawyer; action under IP, PDPA, privilege or contempt-of-court law as applicable.

## 4. Self-help material for SRPs at SCT

- **"A Guide to Small Claims"** (PDF, State Courts, 23 pages, info correct as of 1 Dec 2022) — https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/docs/default-source/civil-docs/sct_guide_to_small_claims.pdf — covers Eligibility, CJTS, Filing, After a claim is filed, Appeals, Language (free interpretation for Mandarin/Hokkien/Chinese dialects, Tamil, Malay; other languages at own cost/arrangement).
- **judiciary.gov.sg "Small claims" hub** (https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/civil/small-claims) links to 7 self-help guide pages: "File a small claim", "Respond to a small claim", "Understand the outcomes", "Going to court", "Set aside orders", "Execute or enforce orders", "Appeal decisions". Also carries a scam warning (court officers never ask for money transfers/bank details by phone).
- **Pre-filing assessment tool**: confirmed to exist. Guide p.6: "Before filing a claim, please attempt a pre-filing assessment in the CJTS." It is a required step before the Claim Form: you complete assessment questions, submit, and get a "pre-filing ID" needed to start the actual claim form (Guide p.11, steps 5–6). I could not access the CJTS portal itself (it requires Singpass login) so I cannot report the exact assessment questions asked inside it — only that it exists and precedes filing.
- **Claim form fields** (from Guide, Chapter 3 steps + Chapter 1 "What documents should I upload?"): Claimant details; Respondent details (the party being claimed against); claim amount; supporting documents uploaded as PDF (contracts, invoices, receipts, letters, emails, photographs, tenancy agreement + stamp duty certificate for tenancy claims, ACRA business profile for corporate parties, Letter of Authorisation for corporate representatives). I did not find a public field-by-field CJTS claim-form breakdown for "what happened" / "remedy sought" narrative fields — the Guide describes documents required, not the literal on-screen labels of the claim form (which sits behind Singpass login and I could not access).
- Court proceedings are in English; free interpretation for Mandarin/Hokkien/Chinese dialects, Tamil, Malay (Guide, Chapter 6, p.23).

## 5. Statistics and reported cases

### Caseload
- **2023 State Courts overall**: "178,080 cases filed in State Courts in 2023 and 173,454 cases were disposed of," clearance rate 97%. Source found via search snippet referencing https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/who-we-are/statistics/caseload-statistics-2023 — **I did not independently fetch and confirm this page's exact wording**; treat as reported-by-search, not directly verified.
- **SCT specifically, 2022**: "9,113 cases were filed in the SCTs" in 2022, described as "about 75% of all cases filed in the Community Justice and Tribunals cluster." This figure is attributed (via search) to the Singapore Law Gazette article "Small Claims, Big Impact" — https://lawgazette.com.sg/feature/small-claims-big-impact/ — **I could not directly fetch this page (403 error)**; the figure comes from a search-engine summary of it, not a page I read myself. Flag as unverified at first hand.
- The same secondhand summary states SCT caseload "amounts to roughly two-thirds of all civil claims filed in both the Magistrates' and District Courts combined" — again from the Law Gazette article via search summary, not independently confirmed.
- **2023–2024 SCT-specific breakdown**: NOT found/verified. The Judiciary's "Caseload statistics 2022" and "2023" pages, and data.gov.sg's "Workload Statistics of Supreme Court, State Courts and Family Justice Courts" and "State Courts Annual Caseload Figures" datasets, were identified as the right place to look (https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_afea2ada431ec25fbddc2e1981d2c8c2/view and https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_126d6f58c18f565242425800f88b34b8/view) but I did not fetch and extract numbers from these datasets — say so plainly: **no verified 2023/2024 SCT filing count in this research**.

### Share of self-represented litigants
**Not found.** No official statistic located for the proportion of SCT litigants who are self-represented. (It's structurally near-100% at first instance, since s 23 bars lawyers from appearing before the Registrar/Tribunal Magistrate — but that is a legal fact about representation rights, not a measured statistic about who used a lawyer at the appeal stage or who had informal help.)

### Most common claim categories
- **Renovation disputes**: "About 160 consumer cases filed with the Small Claims Tribunal each year were disputes on renovations" — again sourced only via a search-engine summary of the Law Gazette "Small Claims, Big Impact" article, which I could not fetch directly (403). Same article reportedly states CASE received "about 120 consumer complaints per year" (2017–2023) about prepayment losses on incomplete renovation work, average contract value around $7,400. **Treat both figures as unverified at first hand** — I read a search summary, not the source page's actual text.
- No verified figures found for "online purchases" or "deposits" as claim categories specifically (beyond the general eligibility list in §1).

### Publication of SCT decisions
- SCT decisions themselves are **not published** — consistent with the general understanding stated in the task; I found nothing on judiciary.gov.sg contradicting this (the Guide's Chapter 5 on Appeals makes clear appeals go up to the District Court, then General Division of the High Court, and it is judgments **at those appellate levels** that get published via eLitigation, not the original Tribunal orders).

### Reported SCT appeal cases found (via eLitigation.sg, fetched directly)
1. **Lakshmi d/o Kumaravelu v Lazada Singapore Pte Ltd, [2021] SGHC 174**, General Division of the High Court, 12 July 2021 — https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2021_SGHC_174
   - Dispute: e-commerce/goods (defective dishwasher purchased via Lazada). SCT dismissed the claim for eczema-related damages, allowed only a refund. Plaintiff tried to appeal without first obtaining leave from the District Court (which had already been denied) and instead filed a fresh High Court case. The High Court held there was "no right of appeal in the first place" and the Tribunal's order was "final and binding on the parties" absent leave — dismissed. (Not a renovation/deposit case, but directly on point for SCT appeal procedure and s 40's finality.)
2. **Khew Kim Kee v Sim Jo-Lin, [2012] SGHC 235**, High Court, 26 November 2012 — https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2012_SGHC_235
   - This is a **tenancy dispute** (landlord sought summary judgment for unpaid rent after tenant stopped a cheque, alleging the landlord failed to provide habitable premises/an SCV connection) but I could not confirm from the case text whether it originated in the SCT or was filed directly in a civil court — the summary I obtained describes it as "an appeal to the High Court from a summary judgment order made by an Assistant Registrar," which reads like an ordinary civil suit, not an SCT appeal. **Flagging this as not confirmed to be an SCT case** — do not cite it as an SCT appeal without checking the full judgment.
- I was **not able to find a confirmed, verified renovation-dispute or security-deposit SCT appeal case** with a citation within the time available. Searches for "SCT renovation appeal" and "SCT deposit appeal" surfaced only general explainer articles, not case citations. This is a gap — flagging plainly rather than guessing a case name.
